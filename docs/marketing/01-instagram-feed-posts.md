```

Here's the thing most starter lists get wrong: they're all openings.

A kid who freezes at the start usually freezes again in the middle, around page four, when the story has stopped being an idea and started being work. "Once upon a time" is useless to them at that point. "But there was a problem:" is not — it hands them a conflict without asking them to invent one.

And the reason a starter works when "just write anything!" doesn't: it removes the decision, not the thinking. They still have to say what changed, what the problem was, what they learned. You've just taken the cost of the first three words off the table.
```

```
Dyslexia Font — one tap swaps the body face to OpenDyslexic. Caveat: it loads from a third-party font CDN. Test it behind your filter before you promise it to a student, because a blocked font just falls back silently.

Focus Mode — full screen, one page, everything else gone, Esc to exit.

And there's a floor below all of that: Story Blanks builds a whole story from tapped word-bank choices, so a student who cannot yet spell "dinosaur" still authors something. There are three templates today. That's not many, and I'd rather say so.

Two corrections while I'm here.

Our own website claims a high-contrast mode. There isn't one. That line is wrong and we're fixing the page.

And there is no accessibility audit, no VPAT, no conformance statement and no standards alignment behind any of this. I'd call it thoughtfully designed for access. I would not call it certified, and if your district requires documented conformance, this doesn't have it yet.

Judge the design choices against your own framework. That's the only honest way to offer them.
```
